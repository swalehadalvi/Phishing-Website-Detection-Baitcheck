    Safety tips

    How to read a web address

    Only one part of an address says who owns the site. Everything to its left is chosen by that owner, and everything to its right is just a page.

    Same words, different owner

    https://www.paypal.com/signin

    Real. The owner is paypal.com.

    https://paypal.com.secure-check.top/signin

    Fake. The owner is secure-check.top. "paypal.com" is only a subdomain it chose.

    https://paypa1.com/signin

    Fake. A digit 1 stands in for the letter l.

    https://accounts.google.com@203.0.113.9/

    Fake. Everything before the @ is ignored. The browser goes to the IP address.

    https://xn--pple-43d.com/

    Fake. Written with a Cyrillic "а" that looks identical to a Latin "a". The xn-- prefix gives it away.

    Habits that protect you

    • Type the address yourself or use a saved bookmark for banks, wallets and email.
    • Distrust urgency. Real companies do not give you ten minutes before your account closes.
    • Never share a one-time code. No genuine support agent needs it.
    • The padlock is not proof. Scam sites get HTTPS certificates too.
    • Hover before you tap. On a computer, the real destination shows at the bottom of the window.
    • Use a password manager. It will not fill a password on a domain it has never seen.
    • Turn on two-step sign-in with an authenticator app or a security key.

    If you already clicked

    1. Close the page. Do not enter anything else.
    2. If you typed a password, change it now on the real site, then anywhere you reused it.
    3. If you shared card or bank details, call the number on the back of your card or on the bank's official site.
    4. Sign out of all sessions and check recent activity for logins you do not recognise.
    5. Report the link using the places on the Threat list page.

    Report fraud

    Report a fraud website in India

    Complaints about fraud websites go to the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, run by the Government of India. The portal takes reports. It does not publish a list of fraud sites, so Baitcheck cannot read data from it.

    Lost money? Call 1930 first.

    1930 is the national helpline for online financial fraud. Calling within the first hours gives your bank the best chance of holding the transfer. Then file the complaint on the portal and keep the acknowledgement number.

    Filing on the portal

    1. Collect your evidence first, using the list beside this one.
    2. Open cybercrime.gov.in and choose the option to file a complaint. Pick the category for financial fraud if money was lost, or the one for other cyber crimes if not. Menu names can change, so follow the portal's current labels.
    3. Register or sign in with your mobile number and the OTP sent to it.
    4. Describe what happened, enter the fraud link and any transaction details, and upload your evidence.
    5. Submit, then note the acknowledgement number. You can use it to follow up.
    Open cybercrime.gov.in

    Evidence to gather

    • Screenshots of the site, including the address bar
    • The full link, copied as text
    • The original email, SMS or WhatsApp message
    • Payment receipts, transaction IDs and UPI references
    • Phone numbers, UPI IDs and account numbers the scammer gave you
    • Your bank statement showing the debit

    Do not delete the chat or email. Keep the originals even after you have taken screenshots.

    Write the complaint text

    Fill this in and Baitcheck writes a clear summary, with its own address check attached. Copy it into the description box on the portal. Nothing is sent from this page.

    Other places to report in India

    • Sanchar Saathi (Chakshu) for fraud calls, SMS and WhatsApp messages
    • Your bank's fraud line, to block cards and flag the payment
    • CERT-In, the national agency for cyber security incidents
    • Google Safe Browsing, so browsers warn other people

    Outside India

    • United States: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center
    • Anti-Phishing Working Group: forward phishing emails to reportphishing@apwg.org
    • Google Safe Browsing

    In other countries, search for your national police or consumer-protection fraud reporting service.

    About

    What Baitcheck is and isn't

    A free, private first look at a suspicious link. It scores the address, explains each reason, and checks it against a public list of reported phishing domains.

    How the score works

    Each warning sign adds points. A domain on the reported list jumps straight to the danger band. The total is capped at 100 and mapped to four bands.

    ScoreBand
    0 to 11No red flags found
    12 to 29A few warning signs
    30 to 59Suspicious
    60 to 100Likely phishing

    Domains belonging to well-known companies are recognised and skip the wording and brand tests, so accounts.google.com is not penalised for containing "accounts".

    Where the data comes from

    The reported-domain list is a snapshot of the active domains in the open-source Phishing.Database project by Mitchell Krogza, taken on . It holds domains, packed into a compact fingerprint index inside this page so lookups work offline.

    The list changes daily. A domain missing from the snapshot may still be new, and a listed domain may since have been cleaned up.

    Limits you should know about

    A web page cannot reach other websites, so Baitcheck judges the address and not what sits behind it. It does not see the page content, the age of the domain, the details of the certificate, or live reports newer than the snapshot. Those appear as "Not checked here" in every result. Treat a clean result as "nothing obvious", never as "safe".

    Questions

    Is the link I paste sent anywhere?

    No. The analysis runs inside this page. Recent checks and flagged links are saved in your browser's local storage on this device only.

    Why does a trusted site sometimes show a few flags?

    Some legitimate services use long addresses, tracking parameters or shorteners. Flags describe risk in the address, so read each reason and judge for yourself.

    Why is "Not checked here" in every result?

    Domain age and certificate checks need requests to other servers, which this page is not allowed to make. Rather than guess, it says so.

    Does Baitcheck use data from the cyber crime portal?

    No. The portal accepts complaints but does not publish a list of fraud sites or offer a feed, so nothing from it can be loaded here. Baitcheck uses the open Phishing.Database snapshot instead. To get a scam investigated, file it on the portal using the Report fraud page.

    Can I rely on this instead of antivirus or browser warnings?

    No. Use it alongside them. Your browser's built-in protection and a mail filter see things this page cannot.
